Dictation Principle

The nurse should speak naturally. The system should not force a rigid form during dictation. It should listen for clinically useful handover cues and then arrange them under standard headings.

Nursing Shift Handover - Sample Format

Identification

  • Patient name, room/bed, age, gender, UHID if available.
  • Primary reason for admission or current working problem if dictated.

Current Status

  • Present condition, sensorium, comfort, mobility, feeding, urine/stool, oxygen or device support.
  • Only mention normal findings if the nurse specifically states them.

Events / Changes This Shift

  • Fever, pain, breathlessness, deterioration, investigations sent, doctor informed, medication administered.
  • Patient response after nursing care or treatment if dictated.

WATCH

  • Risks or red flags the next nurse must monitor.
  • Escalation concerns such as altered sensorium, fall risk, respiratory distress, bleeding, urine output, or worsening pain.

NEXT SHIFT / PENDING

  • Pending investigations, reports, medications, procedures, review, transfer, or follow-up tasks.
  • What must be handed over clearly to avoid missed care.

Cleaned EMR-Derived Handover Templates

These templates are rewritten from the supplied EMR downloads as generic Nurse WOYZ formats. They should not carry hospital names, patient names, doctor names, phone numbers, or identifiers from the downloaded files.

Nursing Shift Handover

Use at the end of a shift for routine ward continuity.

Headings

  • Identification and diagnosis or reason for admission.
  • Current status: vitals, sensorium, pain, feeding, elimination, mobility, oxygen or device support.
  • Events and changes during the shift.
  • WATCH: risks, red flags, and escalation points.
  • NEXT SHIFT / PENDING: reports, medications, procedures, reviews, and follow-up.

In-House Transfer Handover

Use before transfer to ICU, ward, OT, cath lab, imaging, or another unit.

Headings

  • Transfer from and transfer to.
  • Reason for transfer and current stability.
  • Lines, tubes, drains, oxygen, monitoring, and equipment.
  • Documents, consent, reports, files, medications, and infusions sent with patient.
  • Receiving staff handover and pending actions.

Neurology Handover

Use when neurological observation is a major part of the next shift's work.

Headings

  • Consciousness, orientation, GCS or sensorium trend.
  • Pupils, limb power, speech, swallowing, seizure risk, and aspiration risk.
  • Neuro observation frequency and comparison with previous shift.
  • Safety precautions: fall, aspiration, seizure, and restraint observation if applicable.
  • Escalation triggers: drop in GCS, new weakness, seizure, unequal pupils, worsening headache, vomiting, or sudden confusion.

DVT / Anticoagulation Watch

Use when DVT risk, suspected DVT, prophylaxis, or anticoagulation is part of nursing care.

Headings

  • Reason for reassessment and current DVT risk category.
  • Limb pain, swelling, warmth, mobility, and mechanical prophylaxis.
  • Anticoagulant status, bleeding watch, and contraindications if dictated.
  • Pending review, Doppler, investigation, or medication instruction.
  • Escalation for breathlessness, chest pain, sudden limb swelling, or bleeding.

IP Case Sheet Snapshot

Use when the nurse or supervisor needs a compact patient overview.

Headings

  • Patient identification, allergy status if known, and current clinical problem.
  • Vital trend and abnormal findings.
  • Active nursing concerns and device care.
  • Medication, investigation, procedure, transfer, and review status.
  • Clear pending tasks for the next responsible nurse.
